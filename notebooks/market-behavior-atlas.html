ETH market behavior atlas¶

This chapter deliberately forgets the 60/60 thesis for a moment. It asks three broader questions:

  1. How far does ETH naturally move, and how long does it take?
  2. How do fees reshape the required hit rate for many target/stop combinations?
  3. Which deterministic signal families survive both an older discovery period and a newer validation period?

Nearby timestamps are not treated as independent trades. Strategy results use sequential, non-overlapping positions and a horizon-sized purge between periods.

1. How much clock does each barrier need?¶

Each cell asks whether either the upper or lower barrier was reached before the stated timeout. No trading direction is selected here. This is simply ETH's movement capacity.

2. Which side arrived first?¶

Zero means the two directions split evenly. Positive means the upper barrier arrived first more often; negative means the lower barrier did. This is descriptive drift, not a strategy.

3. How long did successful movement take?¶

The median below considers only entries where one barrier was reached. Blank or slow cells are a warning against calling a profile a scalp merely because its target is small.

4. Fees bend the supposedly fair coin¶

The chart shows the hit rate required merely to break even with a modeled 10 bps round trip. Small targets and wide stops can demand an intimidating win rate even before extra slippage.

5. The strategy tournament¶

Every dot is one target, stop, horizon, and signal-family combination with at least 20 sequential trades in both periods. The upper-right quadrant is desirable. Dots that are green only on one axis are regime-dependent or lucky.

6. What survived the stability filter?¶

A cell qualifies only when both periods are positive, at least half its neighboring target/stop settings are also positive, and at least half its validation time blocks are positive. This reduces isolated lucky pixels, but does not eliminate multiple-testing risk.

7. Is the winning cell surrounded by support?¶

These are the six-hour squeeze-breakout surfaces. A broad region that remains green in both periods is more credible than one perfect cell.

8. A lucky coin can look intelligent for one period¶

The comparison uses the strongest robust squeeze profile. The fair coin control is included to show why one green period is insufficient.

9. Volatility changes speed more reliably than direction¶

The six-hour 60 bps example below shows how often a move resolves and how long it takes. High volatility makes barriers arrive faster; it does not automatically tell us which side will win.

10. What the atlas changes¶

The strongest hypothesis is slower and wider than our starting thesis

  • Only volatility squeeze breakout produced settings that passed every stability rule.
  • The strongest cell was 100 bps target / 150 bps stop / 6h, not a rapid symmetric scalp.
  • Discovery: 65 trades, +17.15 bps/trade. Validation: 44 trades, +26.44 bps/trade.
  • Validation outcomes were 25 targets, 6 stops, and 13 timeouts. Long and short averages were both positive.
  • The best-looking validation coin cell made +7.77 bps/trade, but the exact same coin profile made -3.97 in discovery. That is why one green period is not evidence.
  • We searched 2,880 strategy-period cells. The result is a candidate for forward shadow testing, not permission to deploy a wide-stop live trade.
